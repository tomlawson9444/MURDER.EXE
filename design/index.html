Concept

The pitch

Neo-Kowloon, 2099. The Murder started out as an underground netrunner cult. Its members wired their nervous systems into CORVUS, a corporate surveillance AI built on crow-swarm behaviour. Now they are something in between: chrome-feathered augments, drone flocks, and a voice in every camera.

You play Vesper Kade, a former Halvorsen Security enforcer with a smart revolver and a cyberdeck. The Murder deleted your partner. You will take them apart one district at a time, one bullet each.

The key art above shows that promise in one frame. Vesper stands on a Rain Market rooftop facing Spire Zero, where CORVUS hangs over the tower as a projected eye. Cyan light comes from her side of the frame and magenta from theirs. That colour split runs through every asset in the game.

PILLAR 1

One bullet. One life.

We keep the one-hit rule exactly. Bosses still die to a single shot. The hard part is reaching their core.

PILLAR 2

Overclock

Our Showdown. Your neural implant slows time, your smartgun locks onto targets, and every shot lands in one frame.

PILLAR 3

Hack the arena

A cyberdeck lets you turn turrets, neon signs, drones and enemy implants against the Murder.

PILLAR 4

More than one arena

Four districts, each with its own hazards, enemy mix and boss. This fixes the biggest complaint about the original.

Identity

Logo and mark

The mark is a crow's head in profile with a slash through it, which sums up the job in one shape. The circuit traces trailing off the skull show that the cult is software now. The wordmark uses a squared, industrial display face, cut by two glitch slices in the player's cyan and the Murder's magenta. ".EXE" is set in the pixel face used in the HUD.

Protagonist

Vesper Kade

Vesper is built to read instantly at 24×32 pixels. Three things identify her from any angle: an asymmetric black bob, a cyan visor slit across the eyes, and a chrome right arm holding the Kestrel. Her long coat has a magenta lining that only shows when she runs, a nod to the cult she used to hunt alongside. Every sprite gets a cyan rim light on its right edge, so the player is always the brightest outline on screen.

Each material uses a 4-step ramp lit from the top left. Emissive parts (visor, smart-link, chest light) are the only pixels allowed to be pure cyan, and they cast a glow onto the floor around her.

The Murder

Enemy roster

Every cultist wears a bone-white beak mask, so the whole faction reads as one family. Each enemy has one clear tell and one counter, and its tell colour is lit on the floor beneath it.

Bosses

Four districts, four executioners

Fixing the difficulty spikes

Every boss gets a 3-second intro card (see the UI screens below) that shows its weak-point tell before the fight starts. Boss attacks use the same telegraph language as regular enemies: amber means an attack is coming, magenta means a hazard. Nothing hits you that the game hasn't already taught you to read.

Districts

Colour script

Each district has its own palette and lighting, so a screenshot tells you where you are. The cyan, magenta and amber signal colours never change between districts, because they carry gameplay meaning.

Level design

Arenas built for fighting

Each district is a hand-built arena on a 16px tile grid. It fits one screen with no camera scrolling, so you never get shot from off-screen. Every map mixes hard cover, low cover you can dash over, cover that breaks, things you can hack, and lethal ground. At kill 50 the layout changes, so the fight you learned early doesn't stay solved.

Rules every arena follows

NO DEAD ENDS

Two exits from every cover spot

Every hiding spot has two ways out, so you can't be pinned in a corner. Each map has exactly one deliberate trap, like the Rain Market car park, and it holds the best cover.

NO SAFE SPOTS

Every spot is watched by something

Each cover position is exposed to a sniper perch, a turret or a spawn door. The tactical view draws those sightlines so we can check every spot.

ANTI-CAMPING

Hiding builds Trace

Stay behind the same cover for 4 seconds and the Murder traces you. A Hexer pings your position and the next spawn sends a Carrion or Shrike to flush you out.

ENEMIES USE COVER

Cover works both ways

Rooks stop behind low barriers to fire. Jackdaws only shoot from perches. A Bulwark is mobile cover for whatever walks behind it.

METRICS

Built to the dash

A phase dash covers 3 tiles. Gaps between cover are at least 2 tiles, so you can always dash through. Lanes 1 tile wide are deliberate melee zones.

READABILITY

Cover reads at a glance

Hard cover has tall front faces. Low cover is half that height with amber stripes. Anything hackable glows. You should know what stops a bullet without having to learn it.

Palette and type

Look and feel

The look is dark, wet and readable. The world is almost black. Light is a gameplay signal, never decoration: cyan belongs to you, magenta to the Murder, amber is a warning, and red means death.

LIGHTING

The scene is darkened with a multiply pass, then every light source is added on top. Puddles reflect the lights above them.

VFX

Glitch as feedback

Hits trigger a 2-frame freeze and an RGB split. Death datamoshes the whole frame. Rain slows to dots during Overclock.

AUDIO

Industrial darksynth

The music is built from stems that add layers as Heat rises. Every gunshot has a crow caw mixed into it. Overclock puts the whole mix through a low-pass filter.

Sound

Audio direction

In a one-hit game, sound is a survival tool. You should be able to hear every threat before it can kill you, even when it's off your eyeline. Everything below plays live in your browser (headphones help). These are rough sketches that set the target for the final designed and recorded assets.

PILLAR 1

Hear it before it hits

Every attack has its own wind-up sound, timed to the same lead as its visual tell. A sniper charge is audible from anywhere in the arena.

PILLAR 2

Silence is a weapon

Overclock drops the whole mix through a low-pass filter. All you hear is your heartbeat and the lock-on ticks, then six shots in one crack.

PILLAR 3

The city breathes

Rain, neon hum, trams and distant sirens never stop. Each district has its own bed, so you can tell where you are with your eyes closed.

PILLAR 4

Music follows Heat

The soundtrack is built from stems. Each Heat level adds a layer, from a lone pad at Heat 0 to the full crow-choir alarm at Heat 5.

Mix priority

  1. Lethal tells: sniper charge, Carrion beeps, Shrike crouch. These never get culled or ducked.
  2. Your gun and dash: loud, dry and centred. Your sounds are the anchor of the mix.
  3. Enemy fire and impacts: panned to position, limited to 12 voices at once.
  4. Hackables and hazards: sign creaks, barrel hum, tram warning bell.
  5. Music: 132 BPM darksynth. The bass and pads pump against the kick. Ducks 4 dB under lethal tells, and goes through a low-pass filter during Overclock.
  6. Ambience: the lowest layer, always there, and the first to drop when the mix gets busy.

Part two

The game design

Research

What Kill the Crows actually is

Kill the Crows is a top-down Wild West arena shooter by 5minlab, a KRAFTON studio. It launched on Steam in August 2023 at £4.29, came to Switch in 2024, and sits around 94–95% positive on Steam. You play Isabella, a gunslinger hunting a cult whose members have turned into crow-like creatures. Its whole identity comes from a few tight rules.

MechanicHow it works in Kill the Crows
One-hit ruleEvery enemy dies to one bullet, bosses included. So does the player. Every encounter is a duel.
ShowdownHold right click to slow time, sweep the cursor over enemies to mark them, then fire at all of them at once. It also reloads the whole cylinder. The Crow Eye Showdown has 2 charges, costs 7, and marks up to 6 targets.
Dodge rollGives a few invincibility frames, and rolling past a shielded Defender lets you hit its back.
Run structureOne arena with endless waves. A boss shows up every 100 kills. Runs are short, and score attack is the long-term hook.
EnemiesAxe-wielding melee rushers, gunslingers, snipers, exploding Bombers, and shielded brutes.
LoadoutYou pick a revolver, passives and a Showdown type. Some revolvers have a secondary, like an underbarrel shotgun. Passives often come with a cost: Shot Glass adds Showdown charges but slowly drains the meter.
ProgressionYou unlock gear by hitting milestones, not by spending currency. One example is "kill 15 enemies using the Bomber in one run".
Later additionsThe Switch version added "Curse" difficulty modifiers.

What players love

  • Tight, responsive controls and fast deaths
  • Showdown feels like a power fantasy every time
  • Crunchy pixel art and sound design
  • Unlocks come from skill milestones, not grinding
  • Cheap, and quick to pick up for one more run

What players complain about

  • Only one arena, so it gets repetitive
  • Some boss fights are sudden difficulty spikes
  • Challenges only refresh when you die, which pushes you to throw runs
  • Not much content in total

Our plan keeps everything in the left column and treats the right column as the design brief.

Core loop

One run, start to finish

  1. Jack inChoose a loadout: sidearm, Overclock program, two quickhacks and up to three implants. Runs start in District 1, or in any district you have already cleared.
  2. Survive the flockEndless waves come from a "threat budget" director. The longer you survive, the more Heat builds up, which adds elites and hazards and raises your score multiplier.
  3. Graze and chargeKills and near-misses (bullets that pass within a few pixels) fill the Overclock meter. Kills and hack combos also refill RAM for your quickhacks.
  4. Boss at 100 killsThe district boss arrives. It dies to one hit, but you have to strip its shields, find the real one among decoys, or wait for its core to open.
  5. BreachBeating the boss lets you choose: cash out and bank your score, or breach into the next district with your Heat carried over for a higher multiplier.
  6. Flatline and unlockMilestones unlock new gear between runs, such as "hack 3 Carrions into each other in one run". There is no currency grind. Challenges refresh at the start of each run, not on death, so nobody has to throw a run.

Build plan

How we build it

DecisionRecommendationWhy
EngineGodot 4 (GDScript)It is free with no royalties, has an excellent pixel-perfect 2D pipeline, 2D lights with normal maps, and exports to Windows, Mac, Linux and Steam Deck. Steam integration is available through GodotSteam.
Fast prototypeBrowser build (Phaser or plain canvas)Lets us test the feel of one-hit kills, dashing and Overclock in days, before committing to production.
Art toolsAseprite + LaigterAseprite is the standard for pixel sprites and animation. Laigter generates normal maps for neon lighting.
AudioFMOD (free indie tier) or Godot's built-in busesNeeded for the layered Heat music and Overclock low-pass effect.
DataGodot Resources (.tres)Every weapon, implant, hack and enemy is a data file, so balancing never needs code changes.

Core systems

PLAYER

Controller

8-way movement, mouse or twin-stick aim, a phase dash with 6 i-frames, coyote-time buffered inputs, and a 1px hurtbox at the sprite's centre.

COMBAT

Bullet pool

Pooled projectiles, raycast graze detection, hitstop, and a time-scale manager that Overclock and hit freezes share.

AI

Enemy state machines

Each enemy runs Idle, Approach, Telegraph, Attack and Recover. The Telegraph state is required and never shorter than 0.35s.

DIRECTOR

Wave director

Spends a threat budget that grows with Heat, caps elites per wave, and never spawns an enemy inside the player's view cone.

META

Milestones and saves

Data-driven challenge list, unlock tree, local save, Steam achievements, and a daily-seed leaderboard.

HACKING

Hackable interface

One "Hackable" component handles turrets, signs, drones and Carrions, so adding a new hack target is a single component.

Before we start

Keep it ours

We borrow the design ideas: one-hit duels, a mark-and-execute slow-motion ability, and milestone unlocks. Game mechanics aren't protected. Names, art and story have to be original, though. That means no copying Kill the Crows assets, and no names taken from other cyberpunk properties (that's why we use our own terms: Overclock, Phase Dash, quickhacks and implants).

The first step to agree on is a playable feel prototype in the browser: Vesper, the Kestrel, phase dash, DEADLINK Overclock and three enemy types in one grey-box arena. That will tell us whether the core is fun before any art is made.
